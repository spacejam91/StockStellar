How to read this

Every number on a card answers one question: is this name behaving unusually today, measured against its own history and against the other names in its own market? Nothing here answers what happens next. Read this page once and the cards stop needing explanation.

The bar, and the tick

under 1.0×
cleared 2.6×
off the scale 9.0×

The thin vertical tick is always at 40% of the track, and it is the level this name had to clear that session. Grey means it did not; blue means it did. A squared-off end with a notch means the value ran past the end of the scale — the bar stops, the number beside it does not.

The scale runs to 2.5× the tick. So a bar at the tick is exactly at the bar; a full bar is 2.5× it or more. Because the tick never moves, you can compare two rows, or two names, by eye.

The tick is not a fixed number. Four of the five levels are set from each market's own trailing 60 sessions, so they rise in a violent week and fall in a quiet one. The card always prints the level it actually used.

The five meters

composite composite_z

Where this name ranks against every other eligible name in its own market today, expressed in standard deviations. Nine pillars are averaged, then the result is re-ranked across the cross-section, so +3.0z means roughly the top 1 in 700.

Look for: the number, not the bar. The threshold is a flat 2.0z and never moves. Above 3.0z is the top handful of a 3,000-name market; 2.0–2.5z is ordinary for the day's most active names.
It has a ceiling set by how many names are in the market — about 3.57z for ~2,800 US names, 2.93z for ~300 Canadian ones. A Canadian 2.8z is therefore nearer its ceiling than a US 2.8z. That is exactly why the two markets are selected separately and never sorted into one list.
RVOL volume ÷ 20-day median volume

How much more it traded than usual. 4.0× means four times its own median session.

Look for: volume is the parameter hardest to fake. A large price move on ordinary volume is usually one desk or a data artifact; the same move on 5× volume is a crowd. When only one meter is blue, this is the one you want it to be.
A thinly traded name has a small denominator, so 6× can be three trades. Check close and the liquidity floor ($5M US / $2M CA of daily dollar volume) before reading much into it.
move today's return ÷ its own 60-day return sd

The day's move in units of this stock's own normal daily wobble. +3.4σ is a move 3.4 times its typical daily range of outcomes — and the sign is the direction.

Look for: the sign next to the LONG / SHORT tag. The scanner flags unusualness in both directions; a SHORT tag with a −4σ move is a name that fell hard, not a recommendation to sell it.
A quiet stock has a small denominator too. A utility that moves 0.3% a day can print 3σ on a 1% move. Cross-check against RVOL: a real event moves volume as well as price.
range/ATR today's true range ÷ 14-day ATR

How wide the day was against its own recent average width. 3.0 means the high-to-low span was three normal days wide.

Look for: this separates a gap that then sat still from a day that actually traded through a range. High range with high RVOL is a fight; high range with low RVOL is a thin book.
gap/ATR (open − previous close) ÷ 14-day ATR

How much of the move happened overnight, in normal-day units. This is the closest thing here to a pre-market signal: it is where earnings, guidance and press releases land.

Look for: a large gap with a smaller range means the market repriced it before the open and then agreed — the news was the event. A large gap with a large range means it was still being argued over all day.
Every ATR-divided number breaks when ATR is near zero. Names whose ATR is under 0.5% or over 50% of their price are now excluded outright, because those ratios measure the denominator rather than the stock.

The rest of the card

fieldwhat it means
qualifiers n/4 How many of RVOL, move, range/ATR and gap/ATR cleared their bar. A name needs at least 2 plus composite ≥ 2.0z. Never all four — that would only ever surface halted stocks.
#1 of 2,770 Its rank among the eligible names in its own market. Three US slots and three Canadian slots are filled independently.
scale tops out The highest composite score attainable in that market today. If a name is near it, nothing could have scored higher.
drivers The three pillars contributing most, signed in the direction the name was flagged. displacement = the move itself, confirmation = where it closed within its range on volume, structure = breakouts and distance from the 50-day, relstrength = versus the index and its sector.
LONG / SHORT Which direction the unusualness points, not an instruction. It is the side whose pillar arithmetic scored higher.
⚠ flags suspect_unadjusted_split means a >35% move on under 1.5× volume — almost always a corporate action the data did not adjust. Ignore those.

When the list is empty

That is the product working. The bars are absolute, in each name's own history, so a quiet day genuinely produces nothing. Over the last 89 sessions the US list was empty on 21% of them and the Canadian list on 40%. A scanner that returns three names every single day has stopped measuring anything.

The amber banner. If a session says degraded fetch, the data provider returned far fewer names than usual and every percentile on the page is computed against a market that was not there. It has happened: the same session scanned locally and on the server produced 3,092 and 1,076 eligible names. Do not read a degraded session — wait for the next one.

What this is not

It is a measuring instrument, not a forecast. score_pct is a percentile of criteria strength within one session's universe — 92 means 8% of the market fired harder on these criteria. It is not a probability of gain.

Measured honestly on 258,651 logged rows over 85 sessions, names this scanner selected went on to underperform in the direction the LONG/SHORT tag points: 5-day information coefficient −0.023, 20-day −0.031, both statistically distinguishable from zero. The measurement harness is verified — it finds nothing in random data and finds a planted signal at t=+7.4 — so the finding is about the criteria, not the plumbing. One window of back-adjusted data is not proof of anything, and it is certainly not a reason to trade the opposite. Treat the output as "these names did something unusual today, go look at why" — which is what a screener is for. The measured base rates carry sample sizes and standard errors; read those before the score.

Not investment advice. No part of this places an order or knows anything about an account.
